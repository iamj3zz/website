  content: |
    Created by Nagy András and Adorján Zalán, IRIS marks a rare instance where J3ZZ contributes as composer and sound designer rather than in his usual role as new-media artist. Like the organ it's named for, the work opens and closes on light.

    The piece premiered on the stage of the Csokonai Theatre, Debrecen's historic theater: suspended eight meters above the floor, a monumental sphere of mirrors slowly rises and falls, carried by a circular structure whose every fragment — animated with a movement barely perceptible — intercepts a laser beam and scatters it into a shifting aurora of color across the walls of the hall, like a recomposed aurora borealis, or the very rings of an iris.

    The sound score for IRIS was composed in Ableton Live with Max for Live, designed to move in sync with the sphere's vertical motion and the shifting light. Rather than ambient backdrop, the sound has structure and arc — it opens, develops, and closes with the installation itself. Together, sound and movement complete an experience that feels magical, almost suspended.

    The work was exhibited under the title Reflectio at Lighthouse Art 2020 (August 18–23, 2020), a group exhibition of fourteen light installations occupying the interior spaces of the Csokonai Theatre, Debrecen — the building's farewell exhibition before its renovation. Official credit in the exhibition documentation: "music: J3ZZ" (Let It Be Art Agency).

    Of the entire body of work, IRIS is the piece whose visual language — minimal light, continuous movement, economy of means — lends itself best to a large-scale transposition.

    The visual artists subsequently developed the work into further iterations (IRIS 2.0–4.0, 2021–2023) under their EOZIN collective. J3ZZ's contribution is specific to the original 2020 version.
